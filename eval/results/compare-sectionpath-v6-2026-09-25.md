# Paired comparison of two runs

Pooled over 1 question set(s). 'gained' = right only in the second run, 'lost' = right only in the first. The sign test asks whether the gained/lost split could be luck.

| metric | n | first | second | gained | lost | sign-test p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| answerable: correct (a refusal counts as wrong) | 32 | 24/32 (58-87%) | 17/32 (36-69%) | 1 | 8 | 0.039 |
| answerable: a cited source is correct | 32 | 29/32 (76-97%) | 23/32 (55-84%) | 2 | 8 | 0.109 |
| unanswerable: refused (correct behaviour) | 8 | 7/8 (53-98%) | 7/8 (53-98%) | 1 | 1 | 1.000 |
| near-miss unanswerable: refused | 7 | 6/7 (49-97%) | 6/7 (49-97%) | 1 | 1 | 1.000 |

Median latency per set (first -> second), seconds: 1.4 -> 1.3

Intervals are 95% Wilson. A p above 0.05 means the difference is within what luck produces at this sample size, which is not the same as showing there is no difference.
